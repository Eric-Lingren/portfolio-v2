## Context

Standard Metrics is a portfolio monitoring product for investment firms. The Metrics Tab is where a firm sees the numbers its portfolio companies report, one company at a time, laid out as a grid of metrics over reporting periods.

The first version of the tab was mostly for reading. Metrics Tab v2 rebuilt it as a workspace where people could also change the numbers in place. [ERIC: one sentence on who uses the tab day to day, by role, such as an analyst at a firm or a finance lead at a company.]

## Problem

People who work with financial data already know how a spreadsheet behaves. They expect arrow keys to move between cells, a double-click or a keystroke to start editing, and a paste from Excel to land where they put it. A grid that looks like a spreadsheet but does not behave like one feels broken, even when every feature technically works.

The old tab did not meet that bar. [ERIC: what did people do instead before v2? For example, export to Excel, edit there, and upload again. Only include what you can state plainly.]

There was also a product goal on top of the interaction goal. The new tab had to open the Detailed Datum Tracer from any cell, so a person could see where a number came from without leaving the grid. [ERIC: confirm this was part of the original scope and not added later.]

## My role

I built the grid's interaction model. That covered keyboard navigation, mouse behavior, cell editing, paste, delete, and reporting-currency display. I also wired the Detailed Datum Tracer to open from any cell.

## Key decisions

**One state machine for every interaction.** Keyboard, mouse, editing and paste all change the same few things: which cell is selected, whether it is being edited, and what value it holds. I put those transitions in a single reducer-driven state machine instead of spreading them across event handlers. Each input becomes an action, and the reducer decides what happens next. That made the edge cases testable one at a time, such as pressing Escape mid-edit or pasting over a selection. [ERIC: one concrete edge case the state machine caught or made easy to fix.]

**Match spreadsheet habits, not a custom model.** Click selects a cell. Double-click or typing starts an edit. Delete in select mode clears the value. Arrow keys move the selection. I matched what people already do in Excel rather than inventing a new pattern they would have to learn. [ERIC: anything you deliberately did differently from Excel, and why.]

**Paste from Excel as a first-class path.** Many people keep their numbers in a spreadsheet first. Paste had to take a block copied from Excel and place it across the right cells, not drop it all into one. [ERIC: how you handled a paste that did not fit the grid, such as too many rows or a text value in a number cell.]

**Show values in the reporting currency.** A firm often looks at companies that report in different currencies. The grid displays values in the reporting currency, so the numbers on screen match what the firm compares. [ERIC: confirm whether editing happens in the reporting currency or the original one.]

**Ship the core loop first.** The MVP delivered a full end-to-end editing workflow before anything else, and that moved the project into beta. [ERIC: was this sequencing your call or agreed with product? Say which.]
