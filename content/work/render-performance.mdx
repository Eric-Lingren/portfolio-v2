## Context

At InsideOut Development I worked as a software developer from April 2019 to March 2022. InsideOut builds coaching and training software plus a learning management system.

The performance work centered on the InsideOut Development website. The same site served the public marketing pages and the learning portal customers used behind a login paywall. It had just been rebuilt from new designs which was image-heavy and very resource-intensive.

## Problem

After the redesign, the site was slow for everyone. Customers across the app stared at a blank page while it loaded, and render times were far too long. The goal was to bring first contentful paint down to an acceptable level.

## My role

I led the render-time work on the front end, from the first Lighthouse profile to the final check before launch. Other engineers on the team helped with other changes like implementing Suspense.

## Key decisions

**Measure before changing anything.** I profiled the site with Lighthouse before touching the code. The baseline run scored 47 for performance, with a first contentful paint of 4.0s and a largest contentful paint of 5.2s.

**Fix the largest cost first.** The biggest cost Lighthouse found was images. Its opportunities list for that run put properly sized images at about 6.36s of potential savings, next-gen image formats at about 2.4s, and render-blocking resources at about 0.9s, so images came first. They were resized and compressed before anything else.

**Then change how the code loads.** After the images, we converted standard component imports to lazy imports and moved the mounting of components on each route to React Suspense. We made the third-party scripts load asynchronously and deferred the render-blocking resources that were left.

**Ship in phases and benchmark each one.** The changes went out in phases, and we ran Lighthouse as each phase landed. Each phase got its own before-and-after numbers.

**Targeted fixes, not a rewrite.** There was no big rewrite on the table. The work was a series of focused fixes, each aimed at getting the paint times down.

## What I'd do next

Images were the biggest cost, and we fixed them by hand. If I did this again, I would move resizing and modern image formats into the build. Every new image would be handled automatically, so new content could not bring the problem back.

After that, I would go after main-thread work. Total Blocking Time barely moved, from 190ms to 150ms, because the fixes were mostly about images and loading order. The next target is JavaScript cost: splitting the bundles further and trimming third-party scripts.
